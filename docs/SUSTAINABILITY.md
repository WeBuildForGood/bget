# BGET Sustainability — keeping it alive and independent

---

## The principle

**BGET belongs to the community. Your startup may emerge from BGET — but BGET
is not a recruitment funnel for your startup.**

If someone joins believing "I'm joining an independent community" and later
discovers "the founder wants everyone to work for his startup," trust is
destroyed permanently. That single mistake would cost more than every growth
tactic in [GROWTH.md](GROWTH.md) could ever gain.

**How to do it right:**

- State the separation publicly, in `#manifesto` and `#rules`.
- When your startup needs people, you post in `#opportunities` **like anyone
  else**, with a clear "this is mine" label, and people opt in.
- Never use BGET's private channels, member data, or trust as a funnel.
- Members who work on your startup and members who don't get identical standing.
- If BGET projects become commercial, contributors are told *before* they
  contribute, in writing, with terms.

## Three funding philosophies

| Model | What it means | Risk |
|-------|--------------|------|
| **A. Fully free & volunteer** | No money changes hands. Everything donated. | Burns out on goodwill. Fragile. |
| **B. Sponsored commons** (recommended start) | Companies/sponsors fund events & infra; membership stays free | Must protect editorial/member independence |
| **C. Membership-funded** | Paid tier funds operations; free tier exists too | Risk of becoming a paywall club; must keep a real free path |

**Recommendation:** start with **A** for the first 90 days, transition to **B**
by month 6, evaluate **C** only at 500+ members and only if members ask for it.

Never charge before the community is valuable enough that people would miss it.

## Where money actually goes (real costs, early stage)

| Cost | Early | Later |
|------|-------|-------|
| Discord | $0 | $0 |
| Domain + email | ~$15/yr | same |
| Website hosting | $0 (static) | ~$20/mo |
| Bot subscriptions | $0 | ~$10–25/mo |
| Event tools (Jitsi/Meet) | $0 | $0–50/mo |
| Challenge prizes | $0 | $100–500/event |
| Meetups/retreats | $0 | $500–5,000 |
| Legal (incorporation, if needed) | $0 | $200–1,000 |

**Truth:** for the first 6–12 months BGET can run on essentially **$0–$100/year**
(domain only). Don't let funding become an excuse for not starting.

## Funding paths, in order of trust

### 1. Value-first sponsorship (month 6+)
Target companies whose engineers would *want* to be in BGET — devtools, cloud
credits, security firms, learning platforms.

**The ask:** not cash first. Ask for:
- Cloud/devtool credits for member projects
- Prize pools for Challenges
- Space or food for meetups
- Engineers willing to give a Deep Dive

**The guarantee you offer in return:** one logo in `#announcements` and event
pages, and *nothing else*. **No sponsor chooses members, sees member data,
addresses the community, or influences projects.** Say that in writing before
you accept a dollar.

### 2. Member-funded extras (month 9+)
Optional, transparent: a "support BGET" page with exact numbers
("domain $15, prize pool $200, nothing else"). Publish the ledger quarterly.
Voluntary > required, always at this stage.

### 3. Grant & ecosystem programs (month 12+)
Open-source foundations, startup ecosystem grants, university partnerships,
NGO technology partnerships. Good fit for BGET because you're producing public good.

### 4. Membership tier (evaluate at 500+ members)
Only if members themselves ask. Structure if adopted:

- **Free tier (permanent):** all discussion, events, Build Nights, application path.
- **Supported tier (~$5–10/mo or annual):** funds prizes, meetups, and a
  micro-grant pool for member projects. Never gatekeeps community belonging.
- **Hard rule:** nobody is ever locked out for inability to pay. Scholarship
  path, no questions asked.

### 5. BGET Labs commercial projects (the long game)
The healthiest long-term model: some Labs projects become products/services
with revenue shared transparently with contributors. This is where your startup
ambition and the community's interest can **genuinely align** — openly, with
equity/revenue terms agreed up front.

## Impact vehicles beyond BGET (nonprofit, handoff, community ownership)

BGET **discovers, researches, designs, and builds.** Deployment, funding, and
operations may belong to a different vehicle — pick the one that fits the
problem and the people affected (see [PROBLEM-COMMONS.md](PROBLEM-COMMONS.md) §10):

- **A separate nonprofit** can deploy, fund, partner, and serve — while BGET
  stays a community. Keep them organisationally distinct until a real legal
  structure exists. Safe public wording until then:

  > *Some BGET projects may be supported or deployed through independent
  > nonprofit partners, including initiatives established by members of the
  > BGET community.*

- **BGET never claims to own every solution.** Open source, research, a
  startup, a government/NGO partnership, or community ownership are all
  healthy endings. "BGET isn't the destination. Impact is."
- **The nonprofit's beneficiaries are not BGET's members.** Field partners and
  affected communities deal with the deploying vehicle, not with the Discord.

## Independence guardrails (write these down now)

1. No sponsor ever gets member data or moderation influence.
2. No sponsor can dictate topics, ban anyone, or attend private channels.
3. No pay-to-join. Money buys support, never belonging.
4. Financials published quarterly to members, from day one of taking money.
5. BGET never endorses a member's commercial venture implicitly — endorsements
   are explicit, labelled, and rare.
6. If a sponsor's values conflict with the manifesto, decline the money.

## Anti-burnout (the real sustainability risk)

The most likely way BGET fails in year one is **you** running out of energy.

- **Rotate event hosting from week 4.** You are not the permanent entertainer.
- **Two Stewards minimum by month 3** — no single point of failure, including you.
- **Name a successor in the charter.** Stating it out loud changes how you build.
- **Take visible breaks.** If the founder disappears silently, members think
  the community is dying. Announce it; the community should survive you.
- **Say no to expansion** when the quality metrics are yellow.

A community that runs on one person's stamina is a project.
A community that runs on shared ownership is BGET.
