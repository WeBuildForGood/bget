# BGET Recruiting — bringing good people in

> You don't attract exceptional people by asking for applications.
> You attract them by being a place exceptional people want to stay.

Operational companion to [GROWTH.md](GROWTH.md) (strategy) and
[MEMBERSHIP.md](MEMBERSHIP.md) §5 (the filter). It answers: **where do I find
them, how do I approach them, and how do I keep track?**

---

## 1. The governing rule

**Recruit one person at a time, and only people you can personally vouch for:**

> *"I've seen this person handle X, and they handled it well."*

If you can't finish that sentence, you're recruiting a stranger's potential.
Everything below is a way to **get to the point where you can finish that
sentence** — not a way around it.

---

## 2. Where good people actually are (in order of trust)

Matches GROWTH's channel order — never reverse it.

| # | Source | Why it works | Available from stage |
|---|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **People you've worked with** — job, open source, college, side projects, hackathons | You've seen them handle things | Day 1 |
| 2 | **People who helped you when you didn't ask** | Character already demonstrated | Day 1 |
| 3 | **Peer asks** — "who do you know who..." | Their judgment rides on it | Day 1 |
| 4 | **Thoughtful regulars** in communities you already frequent (OSS repos, Discords, forums, threads) | You've watched them for weeks | After ~2 weeks of your own presence there |
| 5 | **Member referrals** — one slot per confirmed member, per quarter | Self-filtering: they stake reputation | After member #10 |
| 6 | **Public content** — Deep Dive notes, impact reports, showcase posts | Inbound filtering: they self-select | After ~50 members |

**Never:** buying members, list blasts, mass-DMing other servers, scraping, or
"join my server" broadcast posts. You'll get people; you won't get *good*
people — and the ones you keep will remember how they arrived.

**Don't poach from communities you belong to.** The same rule BGET applies to
members being DM-recruited ([GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) §5) applies to you,
turned around. Contribute in public, get known, invite individuals.

---

## 3. The ladder: stranger → member

Skip no rung. Expect weeks, not minutes.

| Rung | What you do | What you learn |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 **Observe** | Read their work, their replies, how they handle disagreement | Are they good? Are they kind? |
| 2 **Engage in public** | Comment on their work, answer their question, credit them | Do they respond well to attention? |
| 3 **Collaborate** | Do something real together — a PR, an event, a thread, a two-week build | The full picture. *This is where the sentence becomes finishable.* |
| 4 **Invite** | Personal, one-to-one ([invite template](templates/invite-message.md)) | Would they fit? |
| 5 **Filter** | Application → conversation → trial ([MEMBERSHIP.md](MEMBERSHIP.md)) | Mutual fit |

The fastest legitimate shortcut is **collaboration-first**: invite them to
*build one thing*, not to *join the community*. Membership comes up when it
becomes real — and by then you have evidence.

## 4. Messages (adapt, don't recite)

The core invite lives in [templates/invite-message.md](templates/invite-message.md).
Three more situations, in BGET voice:

### A. Re-engagement — someone you knew, lost touch with

> Hey {name} — {specific thing you remember they did}. I'm building something
> around {one line: capability with character, a small group of builders} and
> you were one of the first names I wrote down. No pressure — if you've got
> 10 minutes this month I'll tell you what it is.

### B. Collaboration-first — someone you've interacted with, not yet a friend

> Hey {name} — I've been reading your work on {specific}. I'm assembling
> 2–3 people to try {concrete small thing} for {timebox: two weeks}. Want in?
> No strings; if it works, I'll tell you where it came from.

*(Only mention BGET when they ask or when it works. You're offering work, not membership.)*

### C. The referral ask — to anyone with good judgment, member or not

> You probably know someone I should talk to: someone who builds things and
> doesn't treat people like tools. A name beats a link — I'll do the outreach
> myself.

**Rules for every message:** one person, one message; a specific honest reason;
no pitch decks; a clear out ("no pressure"); and answer any reply — including
the no — within 24 hours ([MEMBERSHIP.md](MEMBERSHIP.md) §7).

---

## 5. The weekly routine (30 minutes)

| Done weekly | Target |
|-------------|--------|
| Public engagements with prospects (comments, reviews, answers) | ≥ 3 |
| New one-to-one approaches (rung 4) | ≤ 2 |
| Open conversations personally answered within 24h | 100% |
| Pipeline updated in [templates/roster.md](templates/roster.md) | yes |

**Cap it.** Never more open approaches than you can answer within 24h. This is
recruiting's version of *zero unanswered introductions*. A stall with a
next-action date is fine; a silent stall is a bug.

---

## 6. The funnel (tracked in roster, reviewed monthly)

```text
engaged → approached → replied → conversation → applied → accepted → active at 30d
```

Honest expectations below 50 members: many won't reply (fine), about half of
conversations won't convert (fine). **The only column that matters is the
last one** — accepted people who actually show up. If "accepted" grows while
"active" doesn't, stop recruiting and fix retention: GROWTH's rule is
*retention before acquisition, always*.

---

## 7. What kills recruiting

- **Lowering the bar because numbers stalled.** GROWTH's red line works both
  ways: if quality goes red while numbers go up, stop growing — and never
  "catch up" by loosening the filter.
- **Announcing instead of asking.** "We're open for members!" is not a
  strategy. Ten honest one-to-one asks beat one broadcast.
- **Ghosting applicants.** Answered <72h; declines warm and personal. The
  declined applicant is either your cheapest marketing or your loudest critic.
- **Recruiting impressive strangers.** Credentials aren't evidence. The
  15-minute conversation exists because résumés lie.
- **Reporting member counts as success.** Never announce a number as an
  achievement ([GROWTH.md](GROWTH.md)).
- **Outrunning the culture.** More than ~2 new people a week without two
  Stewards is how the first ten get diluted.

## 8. Where you are → what works now

| Stage | Primary move |
|-------|--------------|
| **0 → 10** | Personal asks to people you've worked with; reach rung 3 first; you personally reply to every introduction |
| **10 → 50** | One referral slot per member; your events and content start doing the asking |
| **50 → 500** | Cohorts, content flywheel, partnerships with aligned communities |
| **500+** | Inbound applications; you stop sourcing — the reputation sources |

**The honest summary:** recruiting the first ten is 90% *doing things worth
talking about* and 10% asking. Most of this document becomes unnecessary if
Build Night runs every week and the impact reports are real — people join what
they can see working.

## 9. Stage 0 — when you can't think of anyone

The honest starting position: two people from Discord who might join, a few
acquaintances who probably won't stay active, and you. That is not nothing —
but it's far from ten. What to do:

### 1. Use what you have anyway

- The 2 Discord people are your **seedlings**. Treat them as the founding cohort.
- The acquaintances each get **one honest, specific ask** — a concrete
  invitation ("Build Night, Thursday, we're building X") costs nothing.
  "Not interested" usually means "not asked concretely."
- A local base also keeps the monthly Impact day *physical and real*. An
  all-stranger, all-remote community has no room to stand in.

### 2. The gate is character, not passport

Wanting BGET international is the vision — every country, disciplines mixed.
But *wanting foreigners because foreigners look impressive* is flag collecting:
ten nationalities in a quiet server is worse than ten active people from one
city, and GROWTH.md already banned that pattern ("never announce a number as an
achievement").

International isn't something you recruit — it's something that **happens when
your work is public, in English, and useful.** People abroad find published
work, not invitations.

### 3. Where proactive strangers actually are

Every place below is organised around *doing*, so proactivity is pre-filtered:

| Where | What you do there (first 2 weeks) | What you look for |
|-------|-----------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Open-source projects you actually use** | Answer one issue, fix one bug, improve one README | Maintainers and regulars who help kindly → then a collaboration-first DM |
| **Small project Discords** (devtools, AI, security) | Answer questions 15 min/day, no pitch | People who answer better than you, months in |
| **Online hackathons** (Devpost, MLH) | Join a team, ship in a weekend | Teammates who *finish* — proactivity by revealed preference |
| **Hacker News / Lobste.rs / niche forums** | Substantive comments on things you actually know | Writers who argue fairly and cite |
| **Build-in-public spaces** (Indie Hackers, X, Polywork) | Post your own small work; comment usefully on others' | People who show receipts |
| **Meetups / GDG / conferences** (local or online) | Attend, ask one good question, follow up | People who show up on evenings — already proactive |

**Signals that predict a good member:** helps strangers for free · ships and
shows receipts · disagrees politely · still there three months later · asks
good questions.

**Red flag:** excited about *joining things*, never about what *they're* building.

### 4. The 30-day version

- **Week 1:** contact the seedlings; make the specific asks to acquaintances;
  pick **one** open-source project and one community; start being useful daily.
- **Weeks 2–3:** publish one small artifact — a fix, a writeup, a build log.
  Work travels; you don't have to DM the world.
- **Week 4:** collaboration-first DMs to 2–3 people whose work you've already
  engaged with (§4B).
- **Success:** one stranger you've never met replies with genuine interest, and
  one of your two shows up **twice**. That's Stage 0 cleared.

**Language note:** perfect English is not required. Specific, respectful and
honest beats fluent-and-generic every time — and async-first programming
([ACTIVITIES.md](ACTIVITIES.md), Think runs all week) is what makes time zones
survivable for an international roster.
