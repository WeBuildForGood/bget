# BGET Problem Commons — problems come here

> The world doesn't have a shortage of problems. It has a shortage of people
> willing and capable of solving them — across disciplines, borders, and status.

The community answers *who*. The Problem Commons answers *what for*. Together
they are the point of BGET:

| Part | Question |
|------|----------|
| **Problem Commons** (this doc) | What should we work on? |
| **People** ([PEOPLE.md](PEOPLE.md)) | Who can help? |
| **BGET Labs** | How do we solve it? |
| **Impact** | Did it actually help? |

---

## 1. The loop

```text
THE WORLD
   │
   ▼
SUBMIT        anyone, anywhere, brings a problem
   │
   ▼
UNDERSTAND    is it real, who is affected, what's been tried, what's missing
   │
   ▼
ASSEMBLE      find the disciplines the problem actually needs
   │
   ▼
LAB           a team researches, designs, prototypes, tests
   │
   ▼
VALIDATE      does it work outside a document?
   │
   ▼
DEPLOY        can it be implemented where the affected people are?
   │
   ▼
IMPACT        who benefited, what changed, what did we learn
   │
   ▼
SHARE         open the knowledge where appropriate
   │
   └──────────► back to THE WORLD
```

No problem in, no loop. A loop without deployment is a study group. Deployment
without learning is a one-off.

## 2. What we promise

**Never say:** "Submit your problem and BGET will solve it."

**Always say:** "Submit a problem. BGET will help determine whether, how, and
with whom it can be meaningfully worked on."

There are more problems than any person or organisation can solve. Ours is to
find the ones we can meaningfully contribute to — and to answer every one
honestly.

**The capacity rule.** This is the Problem Commons version of *zero unanswered
introductions* ([PLAN-90-DAYS.md](PLAN-90-DAYS.md)):

- Every submission gets a **human answer within 72 hours** — framing it, parking
  it, or a warm decline with a reason.
- If submissions outpace reviews, **close intake honestly** ("at capacity —
  reopens {date}") instead of letting them rot unanswered. An unanswered
  submission is a broken promise; a closed door is not.

## 3. Three doors, three standards

**Submission is open. Membership is not.** Anyone in the world can bring a
problem; joining BGET stays behind the application, conversation, and trial;
joining a Lab depends on what the project needs. The full table and the
reasoning live in [MEMBERSHIP.md](MEMBERSHIP.md) — the site should carry the
short version as *"Why membership is selective."*

## 4. The intake

Structured form: [templates/problem-submission.md](templates/problem-submission.md).

Design notes (same logic as the membership application):

- **Under 10 minutes.** Long forms select for persistence, not seriousness.
- **"What would success look like?"** forces concreteness. "Fix the water" is
  not a problem statement; "fewer families boiling unsafe water" is.
- **"What has already been tried?"** prevents us reinventing existing solutions.
- **"Why might BGET be useful here?"** keeps this from becoming a generic forum:
  interdisciplinary angles, neglected problems, research gaps. We ask it of
  ourselves at review time too (§5).
- **"What can you contribute?"** changes the dynamic from *"here is my problem"*
  to *"here is our starting point."* The submitter is often the most valuable
  person on the eventual team — sometimes the only local one.
- **Visibility is a field** (public / review-only / private), not an assumption.
  See §9.

## 5. How problems are chosen — no upvotes

Voting picks popularity, not importance. A meme problem with 14,000 votes would
bury a rural healthcare problem with 4. **No public voting, ever.**

A submission is weighed on:

| Dimension | The question |
|-----------|--------------|
| Severity | How bad is it for the people living it? |
| People affected | How many, and how underserved? |
| Urgency | Does delay make it worse? |
| Evidence | Do we know it's real? |
| Neglectedness | Is anyone credible already on it? |
| Feasibility | Can small teams actually move it? |
| Expertise available | Do the needed disciplines exist among or near us? |
| Testability | Can we find out whether a solution worked? |
| Potential impact | If it works, does it matter? |
| Scalability | Could it help far beyond the first site? |
| Safety & ethics | Could working on this harm anyone? Legally, physically, socially? |

The decision is one of three: **decline** (warmly, personally — same standard as
[membership rejections](MEMBERSHIP.md)), **park** (written backlog, visible to
the submitter), or **frame** (assign a Problem Steward, move to §7). Criteria
are public; every decision comes with a reason.

## 6. Lifecycle

```text
01 Submitted → 02 Reviewed → 03 Framed → 04 Research → 05 Lab forms
→ 06 Prototype → 07 Pilot → 08 Deployment → 09 Impact → 10 Learn
```

Rules:

- Every problem shows its current stage. Status is never a mystery.
- **Paused** and **archived** are honest endings — like GOVERNANCE's "closed
  honestly" for projects. A closed problem with a written reason beats an
  abandoned problem with a green badge.
- No stage changes without the steward's sign-off (§7).
- Stage 10 is mandatory: what worked, what failed, what the next team should
  know. That writeup goes to the knowledge archive.

## 7. Problem Steward

Every active problem gets one. Not a manager — **the person who keeps the
problem from being forgotten.**

Their job:

- Understand the problem better than anyone on the team
- Bring in the right disciplines and keep them talking to each other
- Document discoveries as they happen
- Keep the team moving between meetings
- Stay in touch with whoever submitted it
- Keep the solution aimed at the actual people affected

**Naming note:** a Problem Steward is *not* the Discord `@Steward` moderation
role ([DISCORD.md](DISCORD.md), [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)). It's a
per-problem responsibility anyone can earn by taking one and following through
— VALUES.md §7, ownership without empire-building.

**No steward, no stage change.** This role exists to kill one failure mode:
*"everyone thought someone else was doing it."*

## 8. Framing — the step people skip

Before any Lab forms, someone answers:

1. Is this actually a problem — verified, not assumed?
2. Who exactly is affected? (Names, places, counts — not "society.")
3. What causes it?
4. What has already been tried, and why did it fail?
5. What constraints bound us? (Budget, geography, power, maintenance, local
   skills, regulation, environment.)
6. What knowledge is missing?
7. What does success look like, measurably?
8. **Is someone from the affected community in this conversation?**

**Local knowledge is expertise.** Someone living the problem outranks ten
experts who aren't. It gets equal weight in credit ([PEOPLE.md](PEOPLE.md) §5)
and a seat at framing — not a footnote.

---

## 9. Safety, privacy, moderation

- **Visibility flag on every submission:** Public / BGET review only / Private.
  Nothing marked private is published, quoted, indexed, or shared. Enforced per
  [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) §7 — same report path as member reports.
- **Sensitive content** (security vulnerabilities, personal data, legal
  exposure) never auto-published, even if marked public — a human decides.
- **Spam and "build my app for me"** get a warm, firm decline. The intake
  protects the community the way the application does.
- A problem's visibility can be changed later, on the submitter's request.

## 10. Where solutions go

BGET does not dictate the vehicle. The right one depends on the problem and the
people affected:

| Path | Fits when |
|------|-----------|
| **Open source** | The knowledge is the product |
| **Research / publication** | The answer isn't built yet |
| **Nonprofit deployment** | Public benefit, needs funding and field partners |
| **Startup** | A sustainable commercial solution genuinely exists |
| **Government / NGO partnership** | Public infrastructure or scale is involved |
| **Handed to an existing org** | Someone is already equipped — let them |
| **Community-owned** | The affected people should hold it |

Commercial terms from any BGET-originated project are agreed **before**
contribution, not after — [SUSTAINABILITY.md](SUSTAINABILITY.md).

## 11. Credit

Every problem and Lab keeps an attribution record: originator, steward,
research, engineering, field work, testing, documentation — dated, permanent.
Full rules in [PEOPLE.md](PEOPLE.md) §6.

**"BGET solved it" is never the complete sentence.**

| **D** | Search, contributor profiles, matching, map | Only when C is boringly reliable |

The rule behind the table: **never open an intake you can't answer.** An
unanswered pipeline is worse than no pipeline.

## 13. Anti-patterns

- **Counting submissions as an achievement.** GROWTH.md: never announce a
  number as an achievement. "1,284 problems submitted" means nothing;
  "one problem deployed, 2,500 people drinking safer water" means everything.
- **Fake numbers.** Zero submitted and zero deployed are honest numbers. Grow
  them truthfully.
- **Building the map and matching engine while framing still happens in a
  spreadsheet.** Automate the boring part last.
- **Letting the portal become a free-labor request line.** §5 exists for this.
- **Taking a problem because it's famous**, not because it's right for us.
- **Solving for people who weren't in the room.** They're in the framing (§8)
  or the answer isn't ready.
