# BGET Growth — 10 → 50 → 500 → 5,000

> Growth should be a **consequence of culture**, never a substitute for it.

Each stage has a different job. The most common failure is trying to do stage-3
tactics with stage-1 capacity.

---

## Stage 0 → 10: *Recruit personally*

**Goal:** 10 people you'd be proud to work with, who actually show up.
**Timeline:** 4–8 weeks. Do not rush this.

- Recruit **one at a time**, from people you have actually interacted with.
- Rule: *"I've seen this person handle X, and they handled it well."*
- Each one goes through conversation + 30-day trial.
- You personally reply to every introduction they post.
- You personally run every event.

**Success signal:** of your 10, at least 6 are active weekly and at least 3
have helped someone else without being asked.

**Failure signal:** you have 10 names but only 2 talk. Stop inviting. Fix retention first.

**What you're really doing:** writing the culture in invisible ink. Every
interaction now is the template for how BGET treats people forever.

---

## Stage 10 → 50: *Prove the rituals*

**Goal:** a server that runs itself at least some of the time.
**Timeline:** 2–4 months.

- Weekly Build Night is **reliably attended** (target: 6+ people/session).
- Introduce the **one referral slot per quarter** per member. That's your only
  growth channel — it self-filters because members stake their own reputation.
- 2–3 members besides you are hosting events.
- First Challenge and first Impact day completed.
- Charter reviewed once with member input.

**Success signal:** a week happens where you're offline and the server is still alive.

**Failure signal:** every thread has one reply, and it's yours.

**Hiring:** first 1–2 `@Steward` mods, chosen from people who *already* moderate
informally (they de-escalate, they welcome newcomers, they don't gossip).

---

## Stage 50 → 500: *Systematise*

**Goal:** quality survives without your personal attention on every thread.
**Timeline:** 6–12 months.

- **Application + trial become real** — bots handle logistics, humans still review.
  Target: every application read by a person within 72h.
- Unlock **`🔒 BGET LABS`**. Projects form, get leads, and ship. This is the
  transition from *chat server* to *things exist because of BGET*.
- **Cohort admissions:** admit 10–20 people per month rather than trickling.
  New members arriving together form bonds faster.
- **Documentation replaces memory:** onboarding doc, mod playbook, event run sheets.
- **Moderation team of 3–5**, with a written escalation ladder ([GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)).
- **Discipline on channels:** archive anything with no activity for 30 days.

**What you must give up:** being in every conversation. Your job shifts from
*participant* to **steward and convener**. That's uncomfortable — do it anyway,
or BGET dies with your bandwidth.

**Success signal:** members start correcting new people using your own values
wording — without you present. Culture has become *theirs*.

**What must NOT change:** character-first filtering. The moment you loosen the
filter for numbers, you start converting a good community into a big one, and
those are not the same thing.

---

## Stage 500 → 5,000: *Scale the filter, not the standard*

**Goal:** size without dilution.
**Timeline:** 1–3 years.

- **Tiered access:**
  - `@Public` — read-only showcase, newsletter, open events
  - `@Member` — full community (application + trial)
  - `@Labs` — project teams (earned inside BGET, never applied for)
  - `@Steward` — moderation and program leadership (appointed by contribution)
- **Ambassador model:** 5–10 respected members in their own cities/schools/runs
  small local BGET circles. They get autonomy; you get reach without centralising.
- **Content flywheel:** Deep Dive notes, Impact reports, Showcase demos, and
  Challenge outcomes become public articles/videos. People see the culture and
  self-select in — that's inbound filtering, which is the cheapest kind.
- **Automation handles logistics; humans handle judgment.** Bots: verification,
  application routing, reminders, event scheduling. Humans: every accept/decline,
  every appeal, every conflict.
- **Reject rate becomes visible.** Track it. A community that accepts everyone
  has no filter, and a filter nobody can see is not a filter.

**Guardrails at 5,000:**

| Metric | Green | Yellow | Red |
|--------|-------|--------|-----|
| Applications reviewed <72h | >90% | 70–90% | <70% |
| Weekly active members | >25% | 15–25% | <15% |
| Events held vs. planned | >90% | 70–90% | <70% |
| Unanswered intros >24h | <5% | 5–15% | >15% |
| Mod conflicts escalated properly | 100% | — | any bypass |
| Member-initiated events/projects | growing | flat | declining |

**The single most important metric** is the last one: *how much happens that
you didn't organise.* That's the difference between a community and an audience.

---

## The rules that hold across all stages

1. **Never announce a number as an achievement.** "500 members!" means nothing;
   "three members shipped an open-source tool used by 400 people" means everything.
2. **Retention before acquisition, always.** Every member you lose to neglect
   costs more than a new member gains you.
3. **Admit in cohorts.** Lone arrivals are lonely; cohorts bond.
4. **Growth channels in order of trust:** referrals → member content →
   partnerships with aligned communities → public posting. Never reverse the order.
   Weekly tactics and messages: [RECRUITING.md](RECRUITING.md).
5. **If quality metrics go red while numbers go up, stop growing.** Genuinely.
   Pause invites for a month. Fix the yellow. Growth will still be there.
